# 4.16 - Final Preparation Notes for English Technical Interviews (Simplified and Easy-to-Read + Life-Saving Phrases + Closing Remarks)

## Document Description
These notes are designed to help you prepare for English interviews in cloud-native, operations, or DevOps roles that involve a mix of English and Chinese.

The goal of this version is not to speak at an advanced level, but rather to:
1. Be able to start talking.
2. Save yourself when you don’t understand something.
3. Stay calm when questioned further.
4. Explain technical concepts in the simplest English possible.
5. End the interview naturally.

---

## I. First and Foremost: What Will Help You Survive This Interview
You won’t survive this interview by being “very good at English.” Instead, it’s these three things that will save you:

1. Life-saving phrases.
2. Ability to identify key words.
3. Short-answer techniques.

---

## II. The Most Important Life-Saving Phrases (Place Them at the Beginning)
### 1. If I Don’t Understand, Please Repeat
Sorry, could you repeat the question more slowly?  
👉 Excuse me, could you say it again more slowly?

### 2. If I Don’t Understand, Please Rephrase It
Could you rephrase the question?  
👉 Could you put it another way?

### 3. If My Brain Goes Blank, Try to Buy Some Time
Let me think for a second.  
👉 Let me think for a moment.

### 4. If I Only Heard Key Words, Confirm the Question First
Are you asking about my role in the project?  
👉 Are you referring to my responsibilities in this project?

### 5. If I Only Heard “challenge,” Confirm It First
Are you asking about the main challenge?  
👉 Are you talking about the main issue?

### 6. If I Only Heard “Kubernetes” and “troubleshooting,” Confirm It First
Are you asking about my troubleshooting experience in Kubernetes?  
👉 Are you referring to my experience with troubleshooting in Kubernetes?

### 7. If I Heard “Terraform,” I’m Not Sure
Are you asking about my Terraform experience?  
👉 Are you interested in my knowledge of Terraform?

### 8. If I Don’t Know, Don’t Just Say “I Don’t Know”
My hands-on experience is limited.  
👉 My practical experience is limited.

### 9. I Understand a Bit, But I’m Still Learning
I understand the basic idea, and I am still learning.  
👉 I grasp the basic concept, but I’m still in the process of learning it.

### 10. If My English Is Average, Let Them Know Upfront
My spoken English is limited, but I can communicate about technical topics.  
👉 My English speaking skills are not excellent, but I can discuss technical matters.

## Fifteen, Closing Remarks / Conclusions

### After Answering a Question
That is my basic understanding of the topic.  
👉 This is my basic understanding of the matter.

That is my main experience in this field.  
👉 This is my primary experience in this area.

That is the general idea behind it.  
👉 That’s the general purpose or meaning.

### When You Don’t Know the Answer
My direct experience is limited, but I am still learning.  
👉 My direct experience is not extensive, but I am still studying it.

That is what I understand at this stage.  
👉 This is my current understanding of the topic.

### At the End of the Interview
Thank you for your time.  
👉 Thank you for giving me your time.

Thank you for the interview opportunity.  
👉 Thank you for this interview.

Thank you for your time and questions.  
👉 Thank you for your time and for asking these questions.

### If the interviewer asks: "Do you have any questions?"
No more questions. Thank you.  
👉 I don’t have any additional questions; thank you.

Could you briefly share the daily work of this role?  
👉 Could you provide some information about the daily tasks of this position?
